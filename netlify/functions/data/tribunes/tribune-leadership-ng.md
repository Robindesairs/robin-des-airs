## The Compensation Nigerian Travellers Are Owed in Europe, and Nobody at Home Is Responsible for Explaining

*A passenger delayed three hours at a European airport last month, flying home to Lagos, was very likely owed a fixed cash sum by regulation, not by goodwill. No one behind any counter in Nigeria was under any obligation to mention it.*

### An entitlement that falls outside anyone's job description

Nigeria has a genuine consumer protection framework for air travel. The Nigeria Civil Aviation Regulations, administered by the NCAA, set out real obligations airlines owe passengers on flights within Nigerian jurisdiction: care during delays, refund rules, a formal complaints channel. It is a functioning system, and it is entirely reasonable that most travellers assume it is the only one that governs their journey.

It is not. The moment a Nigerian passenger boards a flight bound for Europe on a European carrier, a second, entirely separate body of law can attach itself to that same ticket: European Regulation (EC) 261/2004. It sits outside Nigerian territory, outside the NCAA's mandate, and outside the reach of any Nigerian regulator's public information budget. Nobody local is responsible for telling travellers it exists, because strictly speaking it belongs to nobody local. That absence of an owner is the gap this piece is about, not a failure by any Nigerian institution.

### The rule the ticket itself decides

EU261 does not depend on nationality, residence, or where the ticket was purchased. It depends on two facts only: where the flight departs from, and which flag the operating carrier flies.

A flight leaving a European Union airport is covered whatever the airline is. That branch rarely concerns a Lagos departure directly. The branch that matters here is the second one: a flight departing a non-EU country such as Nigeria towards the European Union is covered only if the airline physically operating it is a European carrier. Lagos to Paris on Air France qualifies. Lagos to Amsterdam on KLM qualifies. Lagos to Frankfurt on Lufthansa qualifies. The same route, the same delay, the same missed connection, flown by a non-European carrier instead, falls outside EU261 entirely.

Distance sets the amount, not the fare paid or the class of travel. For an arrival delay beyond three hours, a cancellation without adequate notice, or denied boarding on a qualifying flight, EU261 fixes the sum: 250 euros up to 1 500 kilometres, 400 euros between 1,500 and 3 500 kilometres, and 600 euros beyond that. Lagos to almost any European capital passes 3 500 kilometres, which puts the figure at 600 euros per passenger, each member of a family counted separately, whatever the ticket cost.

A passenger continuing to the United Kingdom meets a third variant again. Since Brexit, the UK runs its own retained version, generally known as UK261, in its own currency and its own thresholds, roughly £220, £350 and £520 by distance, following the identical flag logic on the UK side. Three travellers delayed on the same afternoon, at the same gate, on three different airlines, can end up owed nothing in common at all.

### The excuse offered at the gate is often wrong in law

Airlines commonly attribute a delay to a "technical fault" and present that as the end of the discussion. It rarely is. Under the European Court of Justice's Wallentin-Hermann ruling, a mechanical or technical problem is treated as an ordinary hazard of operating an airline, not an extraordinary circumstance, and it does not excuse the carrier from paying. Genuinely extraordinary events, severe weather, security threats, an air traffic control strike, can excuse it. A worn component almost never can. A traveller who accepts the first explanation offered at the gate is frequently accepting a defence that a court would reject outright.

### A blind spot, not a scandal

None of this reflects any failing by the NCAA. Its mandate is Nigerian aviation, and within that mandate it runs a real, working consumer protection regime. The gap exists precisely because EU261 sits outside anyone's territorial job description: a Nigerian regulator has no reason to publicise a European instrument, a European regulator has no channel into a Lagos departure lounge, and the airline selling the ticket has every commercial incentive to stay silent about an entitlement it would rather not pay out.

That is a structural blind spot, and ordinary consumer-protection principle already has an answer for it: disclosure, not a new regulator or a new treaty. An airline or a travel agent selling a Europe-bound ticket on a European carrier could simply state, in the same confirmation email that already lists baggage allowance and check-in deadlines, which compensation regime applies if the flight runs badly late. Every carrier already knows the answer for every route it flies. Withholding it costs the airline nothing and saves the traveller nothing either, except by accident.

### What is actually at stake

Where these claims are pursued through the French courts, following an assignment of the claim to a specialised recovery service, a passenger generally has five years to act, a long window but not an indefinite one, and it does not run itself. Every year, a meaningful number of Nigerian travellers land in Europe several hours late on a European carrier, are owed a fixed sum in three figures, and go home with nothing, simply because the entitlement lived in a rulebook that nobody on the Nigerian side of the counter was ever asked to hand them.
